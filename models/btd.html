BTD#

The block term decomposition (BTD) in rank-(L, L, 1) terms splits a third-order tensor, such as a multichannel image, into R terms. Each term is a spatial map of rank L times a vector: the term’s spectrum, or more generally its signature across the third dimension.

Use it for images with K numbers per pixel (for example K spectral channels), when the scene is made of a few components, each with its own layout and its own signature.

The model#

For a tensor \(\mathcal{T}\) of shape (I, J, K):

\[ \mathcal{T} \approx \sum_{r=1}^{R} \left(\mathbf{A}_r \mathbf{B}_r^{\top}\right) \circ \mathbf{c}_r \]

  • \(\mathbf{A}_r\) (I × L) and \(\mathbf{B}_r\) (J × L) give the spatial map \(\mathbf{A}_r \mathbf{B}_r^{\top}\), a matrix of rank L;

  • \(\mathbf{c}_r\) (length K) is the signature of term r;

  • \(\circ\) is the outer product: pixel (i, j) of term r holds the map value at (i, j) times \(\mathbf{c}_r\).

The values can have any sign. The factors are stored as A (I × R·L), B (J × R·L) and C (K × R), with the columns of term r in the r-th block.

Parameters#

Parameter

Meaning

dims

shape (I, J, K) of the tensor

R

number of terms

L

rank of the spatial maps: one integer for all terms, or one per term

block_mode

which dimension holds the signature: "LL1" (third, default), "L1L" (second) or "1LL" (first)

Fitting#

BTD.fit uses alternating least squares (fit_btd_als()), with a random or SVD-based start (init="random" or init="svd").

from pybbtd import BTD

model = BTD((60, 80, 6), R=3, L=2)
model.fit(data, init="svd", max_iter=3000, rel_tol=1e-10)
A, B, C = model.factors
